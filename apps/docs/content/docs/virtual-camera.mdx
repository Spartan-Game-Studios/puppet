title: Virtual camera
description: Use the avatar as a webcam called VPuppet Virtual Camera in Zoom, Meet, Discord, FaceTime and OBS.

VPuppet can show up as a webcam called **VPuppet Virtual Camera**, so video-call and streaming apps can use the avatar directly. The controls are in the camera card's **VIRTUAL CAM** section, bottom left of the stage. `W` switches the output on and off.

## What the camera sends

- The **capture view**: the avatar without any interface, with the same framing, lights and scene as the stage.
- **1280×720** (default) or **1920×1080**, at **30 fps**.
- Cameras carry no transparency. With a **CLEAR** background the avatar is sent over the chosen colour (green `#00ff00` by default), so you can still chroma-key it. A 3D environment is sent as rendered, except depth of field, which is shown on screen only.

## One-time setup [#setup]

The panel tells you what your system still needs and offers the right button.

<Tabs items={['macOS', 'Windows', 'Linux']}>
<Tab value="macOS">

Needs macOS 12.3 or later and a signed build (the Steam build is signed).

<Steps>
<Step>
Move **VPuppet.app** to **/Applications** and open it from there. macOS installs camera extensions only from apps in /Applications.
</Step>
<Step>
In **VIRTUAL CAM**, click **INSTALL** on the **EXTENSION** line.
</Step>
<Step>
Approve it. Click **OPEN SETTINGS** in the panel to jump to the right page:

- macOS 15 and later: **System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions → Camera Extensions**, and switch **VPuppet** on.
- macOS 13–14: **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, and click **Allow** under the message about VPuppet's system software.
</Step>
<Step>
The EXTENSION line reads **ACTIVE**. Switch **CAMERA OUTPUT** on (`W`).
</Step>
</Steps>

The camera exists only while VPuppet is sending. Turn the output off or quit VPuppet and **VPuppet Virtual Camera** disappears from every app's camera list, so no frozen or black camera is left behind. If an app shows **VPUPPET / WAITING FOR FRAMES**, the camera is there but no frames are arriving yet.

The EXTENSION line also shows **NEEDS UPDATE** (click **UPDATE**) after an app update changes the extension, and **UNINSTALL** (press twice to confirm; macOS may ask for an administrator password). Removing VPuppet.app from /Applications also removes the extension.

</Tab>
<Tab value="Windows">

Windows 10 or 11.

<Steps>
<Step>
In **VIRTUAL CAM**, click **REGISTER CAMERA** and accept the administrator prompt. This is needed once.
</Step>
<Step>
Switch **CAMERA OUTPUT** on (`W`).
</Step>
</Steps>

Only 64-bit camera apps can use the camera.

</Tab>
<Tab value="Linux">

Linux uses a v4l2loopback device that you create once. The panel shows the command; click it to copy:

```sh
sudo modprobe v4l2loopback devices=1 video_nr=10 card_label="VPuppet" exclusive_caps=1
```

You may need to install the v4l2loopback package for your distribution first. Then switch **CAMERA OUTPUT** on (`W`). On Linux the camera is called **VPuppet**.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Use it in an app

Choose **VPuppet Virtual Camera** (Linux: **VPuppet**) as the camera:

- **Zoom:** Settings → Video → Camera.
- **Discord:** Settings → Voice & Video → Camera.
- **Google Meet, FaceTime, OBS:** the camera / video source picker.

VPuppet keeps tracking, rendering and sending while its window is minimised, hidden or covered.

## No native camera? The OBS route [#obs-route]

This works on every OS without installing a camera driver, for example with an unsigned macOS build.

<Steps>
<Step>
In VPuppet, pick a background. **COLOUR** green (`#00ff00`) is the easiest to key.
</Step>
<Step>
Press `H` for capture view: only the avatar remains.
</Step>
<Step>
In OBS, add a window capture source and pick the VPUPPET window: **macOS Screen Capture** (method *Window Capture*) on macOS, **Window Capture** on Windows, **Window Capture (Xcomposite)** on Linux.
</Step>
<Step>
For a green background: right-click the source → **Filters** → **+** → **Chroma Key** (key colour green).
</Step>
<Step>
Click **Start Virtual Camera** in OBS and choose **OBS Virtual Camera** in your video app.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout title="Transparent capture">
CLEAR makes the window transparent, but whether OBS keeps that transparency depends on your OS and capture method (Linux also needs a compositor). A chroma key is the dependable route.
</Callout>
