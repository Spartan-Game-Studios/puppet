This guide turns a **Genesis 9** figure into a VRM that VPuppet drives with **perfect sync**: all 52 ARKit face shapes, plus the 13 VRM presets for other apps. It uses only **Genesis 9 Starter Essentials**, which comes free with Daz Studio, so you can follow it exactly and then swap in your own character.

Genesis 9's face rig already contains every ARKit expression, under its own names (`facs_bs_EyeBlinkLeft`, `facs_ctrl_MouthPucker`…). Some move bones (the jaw, the eyelids), and a VRM expression can only blend shape keys. So the heart of the route is a **bake**: pose each ARKit expression with Daz's own controls in Blender and save the result as a shape key named after it. Older figures (Genesis 8 and before) have no such set; this guide doesn't cover them.

## What you need

- **Daz Studio** with **Genesis 9 Starter Essentials** (both free).
- **Blender** 5.2 or later, with two extensions (Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions):
  - the **VRM** add-on ("VRM format"): search for it in Get Extensions and install;
  - the **Diffeomorphic DAZ Importer**: from [its releases](https://github.com/Diffeomorphic/import_daz/releases) download the latest source code (zip). In Get Extensions open the **⌄** menu, **Install from Disk…**, and pick that zip as it is. Check that it's **enabled**.
- Diffeomorphic's **Daz Studio script**, which is in the same zip but not installed by Blender: from its `to_daz_studio/Scripts/Diffeomorphic/` folder, copy `export_to_blender.dsa` and `export_to_blender.png` into your Daz library's `Scripts/Diffeomorphic/` folder (create it).
- VPuppet's scripts for this route: [`tools/daz-vrm/`](https://github.com/Spartan-Game-Studios/puppet/tree/main/tools/daz-vrm) in VPuppet's repository.

Pick a **work folder** outside anything you share or sync publicly; everything made from Daz content goes there.

## The quick way

`run_all.sh` runs every step below in order, Daz included (Daz runs headless, without a window). On macOS:

```sh
WORK=~/Documents/vpuppet-avatars/my-avatar bash tools/daz-vrm/run_all.sh
```

It writes `avatar.vrm` into the work folder in about two minutes. `bash run_all.sh 3` starts again from step 3 once the Daz files exist; set `BLENDER=` to your Blender binary if it isn't in `/Applications`. To use your own figure instead of the starter one, build it in Daz (step 1), save it as `g9-starter.duf` in the work folder, and run from step 2.

The rest of this page does each step by hand.

## 1. Build the figure in Daz Studio

In the **Content Library** pane, under your library's **People > Genesis 9**:

1. Double-click **Genesis 9**. Keep it selected for everything below.
2. A **skin**: Materials > Daz Originals > Base Materials > **G9 Feminine Skin 01 MAT** (or any of the Feminine / Masculine skins). The bare Genesis 9 is untextured grey; without a skin you get a grey avatar.
3. A **shape** (optional): in the **Parameters** pane, Full Body > People > Feminine > **Base Feminine Body** at 100% (or Masculine, or a character preset).
4. **Hair**: Hair > Daz Originals > Base Hair > **G9 Base dForce Pixie Hair**.
5. **Clothes**: Clothing > Daz Originals > Base Clothing > **G9 Base Shirt** and **G9 Base Shorts**.

Save the scene (File > Save As) in your work folder. Script: `01_build_scene.dsa`.

<Callout title="Strand hair or card hair">
Daz hair comes in two kinds. **Strand hair** ("dForce strand-based", like the starter Pixie Cut) is made of lines that only a renderer like Iray can draw; it needs step 5. **Card hair** is ordinary polygons and skips step 5, but is often heavy (400k+ vertices); decimate it.
</Callout>

## 2. Export the .dbz

With the scene saved and open: in the Content Library, open your library's **Scripts > Diffeomorphic** and double-click **export_to_blender**. In its **Export DBZ** dialog keep the defaults (Export HD off, Export HD UVs and Default Location on) and accept. It writes `<scene>.dbz` next to the `.duf`. Script: `02_export_dbz.dsa`.

The importer fits every mesh from this file (your character's shape, the clothes fitted to it). Without it, Blender's import stops with *"Mesh fitting set to DBZ"*; the importer's other fitting modes don't support the morphs that drive the jaw and eyelid bones, which the bake needs.

## 3. Import into Blender

The first time only, tell the importer where your Daz library is: in the 3D view's sidebar (`N`), **DAZ Setup** tab, **Global Settings**. Under *DAZ Studio Root Directories*, click **Add Content Directory**, type your library folder, and click OK. On macOS it's usually `/Users/Shared/My DAZ 3D Library`, on Windows `C:\Users\Public\Documents\My DAZ 3D Library`; Daz Studio shows yours in Window > Panes > Content Directory Manager. Without it the import stops with *"Some assets were not found"*.

Then, in a new Blender file, click **Easy Import DAZ** in the same tab. Pick your `.duf`. In the options:

- tick **FACS** (in the morphs list). That's Genesis 9's face rig, the source of the 52 shapes;
- leave the rest as they are. **Merge Rigs** (a VRM has one skeleton), **Eliminate Empties**, **Transfer To Face Meshes** (lashes, brows, teeth and tongue follow the face) and **Transfer To Clothes** are on already, and **Mesh Fitting** is *DBZ File*.

Save the result as `03_imported.blend` in your work folder. You get one armature with the FACS controls as custom properties, and the face shapes on the body, lashes, brows and mouth. Script: `03_import.py`.

## Steps 4–8: VPuppet's scripts

Each of the next steps is one script from `tools/daz-vrm/`. In Blender's **Scripting** tab: **Open** the script, **Run Script**. It works on the open file and saves the next one next to it (`04_baked.blend`, `05_hair.blend`…), which then stays open for the next script. From a terminal instead:

```sh
blender -b 03_imported.blend -P 04_bake_arkit.py -- 04_baked.blend
```

## 4. Bake the 52 ARKit shapes

`04_bake_arkit.py`, on `03_imported.blend`. For each ARKit name it sets the matching Daz control to 1, reads the deformed face meshes (body, lashes, brows, mouth, eyes), and stores the difference as a shape key with the ARKit name. Which control it uses matters:

- **Most shapes**: the `facs_ctrl_` control where there is one (it combines shapes and bones the way Daz poses the face), else the `facs_bs_` shape. `jawOpen` moves the jaw bone 3 cm; the bake captures it.
- **The eight `eyeLook…` shapes**: the `facs_bs_` versions, which move only the **lids**. VPuppet turns the eyeballs with the VRM look-at bones *and* drives these shapes from the same gaze; a shape that also turned the eyeballs would turn them twice.
- **Sideways jaw and mouth** (`jawLeft`, `jawRight`, `jawForward`, `mouthLeft`, `mouthRight`): Genesis 9 makes these with two-way controls (`facs_ctrl_JawLeft-Right`, `JawForward-Recess`, `MouthLeft-Right`, −1 to 1). The figure faces −Y in Blender, so +1 moves toward the avatar's own left: `jawLeft` = +1, `jawRight` = −1.

The system console (macOS / Linux: the terminal you started Blender from; Windows: Window > Toggle System Console) lists every shape with how far it moves; a line saying *NOTHING MOVES* means that control didn't do anything.

## 5. Strand hair to cards

`05_hair_cards.py` (strand hair only). It turns strands into flat **ribbons** lying on the head (their face points away from the head, their width runs across the strand, narrowing to the tip), with a small generated texture that fades the edges and the tip. By default it keeps every 8th strand at 5 mm wide: for the Pixie Cut about 5,400 ribbons, 87k vertices. Fewer or narrower and the scalp shows through; from a terminal, the two numbers after the output file change them (`… -- 05_hair.blend 10 3.5`).

Short hair goes on the head bone. Long hair needs VRM spring bones on chains through it; this guide doesn't cover that.

## 6. Clean up and T-pose

`06_strip_tpose.py`:

- **Removes what only Daz can drive**: every driver, every shape key except the 52, the FACS properties and the `(drv)` helper bones. A VRM can't run Daz's formulas. The arm twist bones now follow their parent.
- **Makes a T-pose the rest pose.** Genesis 9 rests in an A-pose (arms 48° down); VRM expects arms straight out. The script aims upper arm, forearm and hand along the X axis, then bakes that pose into every mesh **including each shape key**. Blender's own *Apply* refuses meshes with shape keys, which is why this is a script.

## 7. Materials

`07_materials.py`. Daz's Iray materials don't export; it rebuilds each one as a plain Principled BSDF, which glTF and VRM carry:

- base colour and normal map from the Daz textures, capped at 2048 px (saved in `textures/` in the work folder);
- **cutout** alpha (glTF *Mask*) and both sides drawn for the hair cards, brows and lashes. Blended alpha sorts badly in real time, and these are single planes;
- the brows tinted to the hair colour (Daz tints them in its own shader);
- the tear and eye-moisture shells deleted (they only make sense in Iray).

The eyes: Daz layers a grey iris over the sclera, which looks pale and staring in a real-time renderer. The script uses one of Genesis 9's 15 combined eye textures instead, `G9_Eyes05_D.jpg` (dark brown), found in your Daz library. From a terminal, pass another one (`Runtime/Textures/DAZ/Characters/Genesis9/Base/Eyes/G9_EyesNN_D.jpg`) after the output file, or `daz` for Daz's own eye.

## 8. Export the VRM

`08_export_vrm.py` sets up and exports `avatar.vrm`, a **VRM 1.0**, with the VRM add-on:

- **Humanoid bones**: the add-on's automatic assignment plus Genesis 9's names (`hip`, `spine1`–`spine4`, `l_upperarm`, `l_forearm`, the fingers…). No `jaw` bone: the baked shapes already open the mouth, and other apps would rotate a jaw bone on top of them.
- **Expressions**: the 52 ARKit names, each bound on every mesh that has that shape, and the VRM presets (blink, `aa` `ih` `ou` `ee` `oh`, happy, angry, sad, relaxed, surprised) built as mixes of them.
- **Look-at**: on the eye bones.
- **Licence fields**: personal use only, no redistribution, no modification (the safe setting for Daz content; change them only if your licences allow).

Everything it sets is in the **VRM** tab of the 3D view's sidebar with the armature selected, and **File > Export > VRM** exports by hand.

## 9. Check it in VPuppet

Import `avatar.vrm` into the [model library](../model-library.mdx) and load it. The bottom bar should say **PERFECT SYNC 52/52**. Open **MODEL INFO** (`I`): its **MODEL CHECKS** list problems such as missing bones, a non-T rest pose or expressions that do nothing.

An AI agent connected to VPuppet can check every shape for you: `expression_sweep` with the ARKit set drives each shape in turn and reports any that do nothing or are on the wrong side. See [Connect an AI agent](../mcp.mdx#workflow).

## Keep it light

The starter avatar comes out at about **180k triangles and 66 MB**: the body's 52 shapes 33 MB (they're stored for all 25k body vertices although only the face moves), the hair 87k vertices, the textures 15 MB. It loads in under a second and runs at full speed on a recent computer, but tracking and rendering share the machine. To cut it down:

- fewer, wider hair ribbons (step 5), or decimate card hair;
- textures at 1024 px for anything you don't see up close;
- remove body geometry hidden under clothes (keep it in your `.blend`, delete it only in the copy you export).

## When something goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause and fix |
|---|---|
| No **DAZ Setup** tab in the sidebar | The DAZ Importer is installed but not enabled (Preferences > Add-ons). |
| Import stops: "Mesh fitting set to DBZ" | No `.dbz` next to the `.duf`: step 2. |
| Import: "Some assets were not found" | The importer doesn't know your Daz library: Global Settings, step 3. |
| Step 4 prints *NOTHING MOVES* | That control does nothing on your figure; check you ticked **FACS** in step 3. |
| Avatar is grey | The scene had no skin: step 1. |
| Hair missing in VPuppet, fine in Blender | One-sided hair cards, or alpha read from the colour: use `07_materials.py`, or set the material to both sides and alpha clip. |
| Eyes look blank or staring | Daz's layered eye: use a combined eye texture (step 7). |
| VPuppet shows PRESETS instead of PERFECT SYNC | Fewer than 40 of the 52 ARKit expressions exist under recognisable names; check step 4's output and the expressions in the VRM tab. |
